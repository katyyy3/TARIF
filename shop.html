TARIF.
Tarif has this funny mix of old-school newspaper energy and a name that literally sounds like “tariff.”

The typeface Tarif carries a clever, subtle irony in bothe its appreance and its name. Visually, Tarif has a strong newspaper-like quality. Its slab-serif structure, inverted contrast and editorial clarity all acho traditional print design, the kind you'd associate with journalism, public communication, and the spread of information. Yet its name, sounding identical to the word tariff, evokes the idea of taxes, regulation and economic policy. The contrast between a typeface that looks rooted in open information and a name that references financial restriciton creates a playful tension. "Tarif" feels. both historic and bureaucratic, scholarly yet administrative, which makes the font memorable and giving it a conceptual depth that designers can intentionally lean into when choosing it for branding or editorial work.
